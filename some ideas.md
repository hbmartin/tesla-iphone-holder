- Stability

  1. Nothing stops sideways sliding. At pad level the nub bases leave 1.45 mm on each side of the phone, which is what holds it in place today. At the 5.4 mm support height the nubs are only 6 mm wide, so the gap grows to 7.45 mm per side (about 15 mm of total slide). Nothing on the inserts replaces that. The nub height was never measured, so the nubs may not even reach above the phone's back.
  2. The camera end has almost no support. Only about 14% of the flat back rests on plastic. In the top third (Y 101–150, the camera end), the camera cut-out leaves two 3.3 mm strips under the rounded frame edge, which is about 42 mm² of real contact. The phone balances on a line around Y≈101. Pressing near the top of the screen with roughly 100–150 g of force should tip it until the lens rings hit the fabric, which is only 0.4 mm away.
  3. Two loose pieces. Each insert is held only by its own nub and the phone's weight. The straight taper only approximates the rounded, fabric-covered nub, which is what V2 failed on. Each piece can sit at its own height or angle, and the phone rocks between them.
  4. Bottom stop and grip. The phone sits 5.4 mm higher against the factory ledge, and the ledge height was never measured. PLA under glass is also much more slippery than the fabric was.
  5. Smaller issues:
     - The right insert extends 6.5 mm past the long ridge, into what looks like the second phone slot in your photos.
     - The honeycomb holes remove much of the left strip's contact area.
     - PLA softens at around 55–60 °C, which a dark cabin plus a warm charger can reach.

  Ideas (consider and evaluate, not decided on yet)

  A. Tilt cradle: keeps Tesla's charger, no magnets. This is my pick if your V1 rule of no magnets or puck still applies.
  - How it works: the phone's bottom sits directly on the pad against the ledge, between the full-width nub bases, so it gets back the factory 1.45 mm side fit. One printed rest under the flat part of the camera plateau, with pockets for the lens rings, holds the top so the lenses clear the pad by about 0.4 mm. Both nubs run alongside the camera end, so this rest can straddle them and stay put.
  - Pros:
    - The gap is close to bare-phone and about 1.6 mm better than V3 at the centre.
    - Solid support at the camera end, and nothing touches the lenses.
    - One small part.
  - Cons:
    - The phone tilts about 3°.
    - The lens and flash layout on the plateau needs measuring.
    - Near the top the phone sits above the nubs, so the rest needs its own small side lips.
    - Still not a zero gap.

  B. Keep the flat lift and fix the stability. Only worth doing if V3 turns out to charge acceptably.
  - Join both inserts into one rigid frame with a crossbar at the bottom (Y 0–40 is clear of the charging circle). This stops the two pieces rocking independently. Con: both nub positions have to be right at once.
  - Low side rails at X≈9 and X≈84, 2–3 mm above the support surface. These bring the side play back to about 0.5–1 mm. Keep them below the side buttons and Camera Control.
  - A shelf under the camera plateau, 2.5 mm tall inside the camera opening, with lens pockets. This supports the overhanging camera end. Con: it needs the lens positions.
  - Smaller additions: a bottom lip with a USB-C notch, silicone or TPU anti-slip on the contact strips, and solid plastic instead of honeycomb under the phone.
  - Overall con: none of this fixes the 5.4 mm gap.

  C. Other possibilities:

  1. Charging comparison with V3, since it's already printed. Charge for 10 minutes on the bare pad, then 10 minutes on V3, starting from the same battery level. If V3 is much slower or doesn't charge, rule out B.
  2. Nub height and cross-section. Use a contour gauge, or press clay over a nub, slice it and photograph it against a ruler.
  3. Lower ledge height above the pad.
  4. Lens and flash layout on the plateau. Take a straight-down photo with a ruler, or press the camera into clay. Both A and B's shelf need this.
